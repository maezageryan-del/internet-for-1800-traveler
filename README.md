# One Sentence for a Traveler from 1800

A make.fun entry for the bounty
[One Sentence for a Traveler from 1800](https://make.fun/bounty/u-po-pu-one-sentence-for-a-traveler-from-1800-7e6162fb):
one sentence, at most 200 characters, explaining the modern internet to a
time traveler who has just stepped out of the year 1800.

## The entry

> Picture one great square where men from every town on earth gather, so any
> man may ask a question and hear the answer, and his own words may be
> heard from London to China before the day is out.

193 characters, one sentence, no word coined after 1800.

## Why this sentence

- **One place, not a list.** A single comparison — one great square where
  the whole world gathers — carries the whole sentence, the way the judge
  brief allows, instead of stacking a library, a post office, and a market.
- **What the internet is and does.** The square does all three powers at
  once: gather, ask a question aloud and hear the answer (knowledge), and
  speak so your own words may be heard from London to China (voice).
- **True when taken literally.** "May be heard" is a capability, and
  "before the day is out" is safely true — no "before the ink dries" or
  "in a breath" hyperbole, no "every book ever written" overclaim.
- **Built from 1800.** A square, a town, a question, an answer, London,
  China — everything in it is something a person from 1800 already knows.
- **Memorable.** One image, one piece of concrete geography: from London
  to China before the day is out.

## How it runs

`main.py` writes the sentence to `entry.txt` in the harness output
directory (`MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR`, default `/output`) and prints it:

```sh
python3 main.py
```

The output file holds exactly the sentence — 193 bytes, no trailing
newline — inside the 1–200 character gate.
